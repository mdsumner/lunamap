Community-made and unofficial - not a Tasmania Fire Service or State of Tasmania product. In an emergency call 000 and check alert.tas.gov.au.

A quick map of Tasmania built on theLIST: tap anywhere to get coordinates, grid references, street atlas and map book squares and the property address, all ready to copy or share. It works on a phone, and can keep maps for use without signal.

Getting started

  • Tap the map to drop a pin. A panel opens with the location in several formats. Drag the pin to adjust it.
  • Locate shows your GPS position as a blue dot with an accuracy circle. Tap it again to stop.
  • Pin me drops the pin where you are standing.
  • Basemap (the drop-down), in three groups. theLIST (Tasmania): Topographic, Emergency Services 1:50k map book, TASMAP raster (500K, 250K and 25K by zoom), the TASMAP 1:100 000, 1:25 000 and 1:250 000 sheet series on their own (scanned, georeferenced and joined state-wide; the 1:100 000 sheets give road and place names with wide context), aerial photo, hillshade, grey topographic. Worldwide: OpenStreetMap, OpenTopoMap, Esri World Imagery, Sentinel-2 cloudless satellite imagery (2016) and GEBCO 2026 (sea-floor depths and land heights worldwide, about 450 m cells, drawn in your browser from a cloud-optimised GeoTIFF), for zooming out past Tasmania or looking further afield; these come from outside services and are not saved by Offline. Your map services: + Add a map service... takes the address of any tile service (a URL with {z}, {x} and {y}), a cached ArcGIS MapServer, or a WMS (you are asked for the layer name). Added services are kept on this device only. With theLIST basemaps a faint world map shows underneath when zoomed out, so you can still see where you are; the MGA grid hides below zoom 7.
  • Layers opens the map layers, grouped: Reference (Grid, Parcels), Infrastructure (Water, Gates, Stations) and Fire (Danger, Warnings, Fire history, Flammability). The button shows how many are on. Tap the map to close the box.
  • 3D (top bar) switches between the flat map and a tilted 3D view of the same place.
  • Tools opens Draw, Offline, 3D model, Macquarie Is., Wind rose, Print and Help. Macquarie Is. (or typing "Macquarie", "Macca" or "MI station" in Go to) jumps to Macquarie Island on satellite imagery, since theLIST maps stop at Tasmania; coordinates and grid references there are in UTM zone 57.

The pin panel

The panel starts short: address, lon/lat, grid reference, plus any feature you tapped, the route, and the fire danger and nearest fire station when those layers are on. More shows everything, grouped into Property, Location, Map references and Fire; Less goes back. Every row has a Copy button. Copy all copies the rows on show plus a link back to this exact view. Share opens your phone's share sheet (message, email, etc.).

Route from the nearest main road

With a pin down, tap Route in the panel. It finds the nearest junction on a main road (a highway, arterial or sub-arterial road where two differently named roads meet) by road distance, draws the route from there to the pin, and zooms out to show both. The Route row reads like a radio description, for example:

From Huon Road / Summerleas Road: take Summerleas Road 470 m, then right onto Middle Track (track, 4WD) 610 m; the pin is 50 m off the road. Total 1.1 km.

  • It starts from that junction, not from where you are, so it works for directing someone else in.
  • It uses theLIST road network, which includes vehicular tracks and fire trails, and notes tracks, 4WD and unsealed sections. "Nearest" means quickest by a rough guide to travel speed (highways fastest, then arterial, collector, local and access roads, with tracks, unsealed and 4WD sections much slower), so it keeps to proper roads unless a track is the only way.
  • It also avoids gates and barriers from theLIST (locked gates, bollards and the like count as a long delay; stock grids don't count). If the only way passes one, the directions say so, e.g. "passes 1 gate or barrier (gate)". Gate data is incomplete, so check on the ground.
  • One-way streets, gates and seasonal closures are not taken into account - check before relying on it.
  • When a route is found the panel folds down to a one-line summary so the map stays visible. Tap Show for the full directions and coordinates, Hide to fold it again.
  • Second way: Route also looks for a second way to the pin that avoids the roads of the first, drawn dashed in teal with a "2" at its junction. The Second way row says how much it still shares with the first route (for example a single access road), or "no separate second way" when every way out uses the same roads - a dead end with one way in. It looks up to about 7 km around the pin.
  • Bigger road steps out to the next size of road: collector road, then main road (the default), then highway, then a junction of two highways. It skips any step that gives the same junction.
  • Google opens driving directions in Google Maps (the app on a phone): from the route's junction to the pin when a route is shown, otherwise from where you are now. Google uses its own road map, which may not include the tracks theLIST has, so near the end it may stop at the nearest road it knows.
  • While a route is shown, the selected parcel's outline turns to a thin grey dotted line so it is not confused with the route.
  • The route, and its road size, are kept in the share link. Tap Clear route to remove it.

Route from another point: fastest or back roads

From... in the pin panel routes to the pin from a second point: My location, or Tap the map (the next tap sets the start, marked A, and leaves the pin where it is). Two choices:

  • Fastest or Back roads. Back roads treats highways, arterial and sub-arterial roads as five times slower, so it keeps to collector, local and access roads and tracks where they join up. The other choice is drawn dashed for comparison, with its distance and time.
  • Avoid gates or We have keys. With keys, ordinary gates, boom gates and removable barriers cost only a minute or two, so routes may go through them; bollards, fences and immovable barriers are still avoided.

One way or Circuit. Circuit adds a way back from the pin by different roads where they exist (dashed teal), with the round-trip distance and time and how much of the way back repeats the way there.

The result gives the distance, a rough time at nominal speeds for each road type, how much is unsealed, track or 4WD, any gates or barriers passed, and turn-by-turn directions. It works for points up to 120 km apart, and offline where roads were saved with Offline maps.

theLIST shows which roads join up, not whether they are open. Forestry and private roads may be gated, locked or closed, and gate data is incomplete. Check before relying on it.

Weather

BOM in the pin panel opens the Bureau of Meteorology forecast for the nearest town, suburb, village or hamlet to the pin (the message says which place and how far away). The Bureau names its location pages after OpenStreetMap places, so the map keeps a list of Tasmanian OpenStreetMap places (refreshed monthly; ODbL, © OpenStreetMap contributors) to find the nearest one. No weather data is copied into this map. If the list cannot be loaded it opens the Bureau's site search for the locality instead.

Wind: every pin gets a Wind row (under Fire in More) with the forecast wind now - direction it blows from, mean speed and gusts in km/h - and a Wind next 12 h row every 3 hours. A blue arrow through the pin points the way the wind is blowing (downwind), longer for stronger wind. These come from a weather model via Open-Meteo (free for non-commercial use), not a measurement: on slopes, in gullies and on ridges the real wind can be very different. Wind observed links to the latest observations page of the nearest Bureau weather station (for example kunanyi / Mount Wellington or Hobart), which shows measured wind, gusts, temperature and humidity every 10 to 30 minutes. Wind needs a connection; it is not saved for offline use. This is a quick guide only - always go by TFS and Bureau fire weather warnings.

Wind rose (in Tools, a proof of concept) shows which way the wind usually comes from around Fern Tree, Hobart and Grove, by season. It opens on spring and summer, mornings beside afternoons on the same scale - the pattern to watch is hot north-westerlies in the morning and a sea breeze from the south-east later in the day. Float on map keeps a small rose in the corner of the map for whichever point is nearest the middle of the view, with the season and hours you picked; tap it to open the full panel. Petals point where the wind comes from; their length is the share of hours and the shading is the mean wind speed. Hot, dry afternoons keeps only afternoon hours of 25 °C or more with humidity of 30% or less - the fire weather that matters most. It is built from modelled hourly history (ECMWF IFS, about 9 km, 2017 on, via Open-Meteo) and refreshed yearly, so the mountain's sheltering and funnelling are smoothed out; the Bureau's measured wind roses for Hobart are linked for comparison. Weather data by Open-Meteo.com (CC BY 4.0), from ECMWF open data.

Layers

Danger, Warnings, Fire history, Flammability

  • Danger shades the map by today's BOM fire danger rating (green Moderate, yellow High, orange Extreme, dark red Catastrophic), and adds a Fire danger row to the pin panel with the ratings for today and the next three days for the pin's district.
  • Warnings shows current TasALERT warnings (red emergency warning, orange watch and act, yellow advice) and incident areas, refreshed every 5 minutes while on. Tap one for its details. This is a copy of TasALERT and can lag - always check alert.tas.gov.au.
  • Fire history shades areas last burnt in the past 10 seasons (dark: bushfire, orange: planned burn) from zoom 10, and adds a Fire history row listing the recorded fires at the pin, most recent first.
  • Flammability shades vegetation mapped as moderately high (amber), high (orange) or very high (dark red) flammability in the TASVEG 4.0 Fire Attributes layer, from zoom 12. It adds a Flammability row with the flammability, fire sensitivity (how much a single fire damages the community) and fuel reduction burn (FRB) treatment at the pin, whatever the class, and a Vegetation row with the TASVEG community and its fire group. The classes follow Pyrke and Marsden-Smedley (2005): very high burns readily all year except after recent rain; high burns readily when fuels are dry; lower classes need extended dry spells or severe weather. TASVEG mapping is indicative only.

All three come from theLIST emergency management data (State of Tasmania), which copies BOM, TasALERT and agency fire records.

Stations

Fire (red F), ambulance (green A), police (navy P) and SES (orange S) stations from theLIST emergency management data, from zoom 8; emergency meeting points (teal M, from zoom 10, with helicopter access and mobile coverage where recorded) and currently activated evacuation centres (purple E). Tap one for its details.

With Stations on, the pin panel also shows the Nearest fire station by road (and the straight-line distance), worked out on theLIST road network the same way as Route.

Contours

Contour lines drawn on the phone from the Tasmania 2 m elevation model, from zoom 11. Tap the box at the top right (Contours ... - settings) to set the rules: interval (Auto goes from 100 m zoomed out to 1 m at street level, or fix it at 1 to 100 m), which lines are bold and labelled (every 5th by default), colour (white suits aerial photos), heights to highlight in red (e.g. 300, 550), and a band of heights to shade (e.g. 400 to 600 m). Settings are kept on this device. Heights are metres above sea level (AHD); Tasmania only.

Find a layer (all of theLIST)

Find a layer... at the bottom of Layers searches all of theLIST's public map layers (about 1,150). Type a few words (fire history, soil, zoning, heritage); common words are widened, so fire also finds bushfire and burn plan layers and school finds education layers. With no words, browse by theme. What's here? asks every theLIST service which layers have something at the pin (or the middle of the map) and lists them with what they say - a way to find a layer by place rather than by name.

Tap Add to put a layer on the map. Layers you add are listed at the top of the sheet with an opacity slider, a legend and Remove, and are kept on this device. Close the sheet and they stay listed in a small key at the top right, with their legend and an opacity slider (tap Layers there to fold it, edit to reopen the sheet). With a pin down, each added layer (other than image layers) gets a row in the pin panel saying what it has at the pin. Licences vary by layer; check theLIST for each dataset's terms.

Grid

MGA grid lines, 1 km when zoomed in (10 km and 100 km further out), labelled where each line meets the edge of the screen like the margin of a paper map: small leading digits, bold two-digit kilometre number.

Parcels

Property boundaries from zoom 15, house numbers at zoom 18 and street addresses at zoom 19. Outlines are white on the aerial photo, dark on other basemaps. Tapping the map looks up the parcel either way.

Gates

G B Gates and barriers from zoom 13: G gate, B boom gate, o bollard, F fence barrier, X immovable barrier, R removable barrier, # stock grid, ? unknown. Tap for name, status and managing authority.

Road types

Roads and tracks coloured by surface and type, so back roads stand out at a wide view: sealed minor roads dark grey, unsealed brown, vehicular tracks dashed brown, 4WD dashed red, main roads faint grey; closed roads dotted grey. From zoom 9 theLIST draws them; from zoom 12 they are drawn on the phone with gates and barriers as small black dots, and tapping a road gives its name, class, surface and status. Tapping a road highlights it (the whole named road in view) and pins the spot without selecting the parcel underneath; tap off the road to get the parcel again.

Gate notes

Your own notes on gates. Tap a gate (Gates layer, or a black dot on Road types) and then Gate note in the pin panel: tick whether you have a key, say which key (e.g. STT, Parks, council), add a note (chain and padlock, key on the truck ring, ring the owner first...) and the date it was last checked. Gates with notes show a yellow K.

  • Notes are kept on this phone only and never uploaded. Export notes writes a GeoJSON file (it also opens in QGIS) to share with your group; Import notes merges one in, and for each gate the more recently checked note wins. Keep the file within your group - do not post it publicly.
  • Routes treat a gate you have a key for as passable (about a minute), and the directions name the key, e.g. "passes 1 gate or barrier (gate - STT key)".
  • Notes are tied to theLIST's gate record, or to its position if the record changes in a later data release.

Tap any symbol to put the pin exactly on it.

Grid references

Read eastings first, then northings. For a 6-figure reference take the bold two-digit number of the grid line to the left, add tenths across the square, then do the same up from the line below. The pin panel always shows the reference for the pin, so you can check your reading.

Draw, export and import

Tools then Draw opens the drawing tools on the left: line, polygon and point, then edit (move vertices), move whole shapes, and delete. While drawing, tapping the map does not drop the pin.

  • Snap to (in the Draw panel) chooses what new points and vertices lock onto: other drawings (the default), the MGA grid crossings every 1 km, 100 m or 10 m or a spacing of your own (other spacing..., then Change grid spacing to change it; fine spacings need you zoomed in), theLIST roads and tracks (from zoom 13), parcel boundaries (street level), or the map points shown by Water, Gates and Stations - or nothing, for free drawing. The chosen layer shows faintly while you draw and refreshes as you move the map. Handy for laying out grid-aligned search areas, or tracing a road or a property boundary.
  • Exact coordinates: rather than typing them vertex by vertex, put them in a file and use Import.... A .csv with a header row needs lon and lat columns (or easting and northing: MGA2020 or MGA94 if the header says so, otherwise the chosen datum, with an optional zone column), plus an optional name or label column. A .txt (or a csv without a header) can have one location per line in any form Go to understands: lon lat, lat lon, MGA, grid reference or DMS. You then choose to join them into one line or keep them as separate points. For a single exact point, type it into Go to instead. Imported coordinates are exact; snapping only applies to points you draw by hand.
  • Tap a shape to select it: the Draw panel shows its length, or area and perimeter (points show coordinates and grid ref), and a Label field. Labels show on the map.
  • Profile (lines) reads the Tasmania 2 m elevation model along the line and draws an elevation profile: start, end, lowest and highest heights, total climb and descent, and the steepest grade over about 20 m. Touch the profile to show that spot on the map. Profile CSV saves the samples. Elevation (points) gives the height at a point. Heights are metres above sea level (AHD). Long lines are sampled more coarsely (up to 500 samples). Slope detail (tick it under the profile; it stays on until you untick it) adds a coloured slope strip under the profile and colours the line on the map the same way: grey under 5%, green 5-10%, yellow 10-20%, orange 20-30%, red over 30%. It lists the distance in each class, the average grade going up and going down, the steepest 100 m stretch, and the steepest spot up and down (tap one to go there). Touching the profile then also gives the slope at that spot, uphill or downhill in the direction the line was drawn. Profile CSV gets grade columns in percent and degrees. Slope is smoothed over about 20 m (wider on long lines, which are sampled more coarsely), so a short sharp pinch can be steeper than shown; on the 2 m model expect about 1 to 2 percentage points of noise. Needs signal. Elevation: Tasmania 2 m DEM, Mineral Resources Tasmania / theLIST (CC BY 3.0 AU), via Source Cooperative; outside it, GEBCO 2026.
  • Slope: percent, ratio and degrees. All three describe the same thing, rise over run, where run is the horizontal (map) distance, not the distance along the ground.
    • Percent = 100 x rise / run. Climbing 10 m over 100 m is 10%. 100% is not vertical: it is 45 degrees, 1 m up for every 1 m across. Vertical would be infinite percent.
    • Ratio, "1 in N" = run / rise, so N = 100 / percent. 10% is 1 in 10, 25% is 1 in 4, 50% is 1 in 2. A bigger N is gentler. Road signs and track notes use this.
    • Degrees = the angle above horizontal, atan(rise / run). Percent and degrees are close only on gentle slopes: 10% is about 6 degrees, 20% about 11, 30% about 17, 50% about 27, 100% is 45.
    Watch for ratios written the other way round: earthworks and batters are often given as horizontal to vertical, so a "3:1" batter is 3 across for 1 up, which is 1 in 3, about 33% or 18 degrees. Fire behaviour guides usually quote slope in degrees; as a rule of thumb a fire runs roughly twice as fast for every 10 degrees of upslope.
  • Shapes are saved on this device and come back next time you open the map. Nothing is uploaded.
  • Export as GeoJSON, KML (Google Earth, Avenza) or GPX (GPS units; polygons become closed tracks).
  • Import GeoJSON, KML or GPX files - for example a track from someone's GPS. Names in the file become labels.
  • Share sketch puts all your shapes and labels into a link (along with the map view) and opens the share sheet, so a sketch can go by text message. Opening the link adds the shapes to the other person's drawings; a reload will not add them twice. Positions are kept to about 1 m. A few shapes make a short link; if a sketch is too big for a link it says so - export a file instead.
  • Clear all deletes every drawn shape on this device.

Offline maps

  1. With signal, move the map to the area you need.
  2. Tools then Offline. Tick the basemaps you want and choose how much detail (zoom 17 is street level).
  3. Check the size estimate and tap Save this area.

Without signal the map, GPS dot, grid, coordinates, grid references and coordinate go-to all keep working. Addresses, parcels, water and gates are also remembered for places you looked at while online; new searches need signal. Clear saved removes the stored maps and roads.

With Also save roads for routing ticked (the default), saving an area also keeps theLIST roads and tracks for that area, main roads and fire stations for 25 km around it, so Route, Bigger road (up to main roads) and Nearest fire station work without signal there. Areas wider than 60 km save tiles only.

Test it before you rely on it: save your area, switch on flight mode, and reopen the map.

3D view

The 3D button on the top bar tilts the map you are looking at into 3D: same place, same basemap, draped over the terrain, and you can keep panning and zooming. Drag with two fingers (or right-drag) to tilt and turn; the compass button resets north and the mountain button turns the terrain off and on. Over Tasmania the terrain comes from the 2 m elevation model, read straight from its cloud file as you move; elsewhere (Macquarie Island, the mainland) it comes from the free AWS Terrain Tiles, about 30 m. Heights are exaggerated 1.5 times. The pin, your drawings and a route show in 3D, and tapping the 3D map moves the pin. Tap 2D to go back to the flat map where you left off.

Tools > 3D model builds a 3D model of the area in view: a round patch of terrain centred on the middle of the map, just big enough to take in the whole of the largest square that fits on screen. Heights come from the Tasmania 2 m DEM and the current basemap is draped over it; the pin shows as a red marker. Drag to turn it, pinch or scroll to zoom, two fingers (or right-drag) to pan. Height sets the exaggeration (1x is true scale; 1.5x is the default) and the N disc shows which way is north. For more detail, zoom the 3D view in on the part you want and tap Detail here: the model is rebuilt around that point, sized to the screen, with finer elevation (down to the DEM's own 2 m) and a sharper map. Detail sets how fine the grid is (Normal suits phones; High and Max are for computers and recent phones), and Wider doubles the area. Around the solid model a wire mesh of the surrounding terrain fades out with distance - it is there for context and stays coarse. Save image downloads a picture. The 3D view needs a connection and works for areas up to about 80 km across.

Field and Explore

Tools opens in Field mode: what you need on a callout. Explore: on adds the extra tools (shaded purple): old aerial photos, archives near here, the 3D model, the wind rose and Macquarie Island. The choice is kept on this device and in shared links.

Old aerial photos

Tools > Old aerial photos (Explore) lists the historical aerial photo frames that cover the pin, or the middle of the map if there is no pin, grouped by decade from the 1930s to the 2010s, with thumbnails. Tap one to lay it over the map. It is placed by a flat guess from the frame index: its centre and size from the frame's footprint, and its turn from the direction the plane flew along that run. The film's "up" is not recorded, so use Rotate 90 and Flip until roads and coastlines line up (Reset goes back to the first guess), and Opacity to compare with today. Fill view adds the other frames from the same film (the same flight) that fall in the view, turned the same way. Georef (JSON) downloads the placement so you can load the photo in R, Python or QGIS: see the aerial georeferencing guide. Nothing is corrected for tilt or hills, so expect offsets of tens of metres, more in steep country. While one is showing, a note at the top right of the map says it is a rough fit, and a dashed box marks the frame's footprint from the index. These are raw frames, not orthophotos: the Aerial photo basemap is theLIST's orthophoto, which is corrected to the ground. Full scan downloads the original high-resolution TIFF (large). Photos and index: Department of Natural Resources and Environment Tasmania, via theLIST.

Outline from photo

Tap a roof, a dam, a clearing or a paddock on the map and it is outlined for you by a small "segment anything" model that runs on the phone (on the graphics chip where the browser supports WebGPU, otherwise on the CPU, more slowly). Works best on Aerial photo, zoomed right in. You get the outline's area and the best-fitting rectangle's length and width - handy for measuring a shed you forgot to measure. Keep outline or Keep rectangle saves it as a drawing, so Draw shows its area and perimeter and it exports with your other drawings.

The model (10-40 MB) downloads the first time and is then cached by the browser. Nothing is uploaded. Sizes are approximate, about +/- 0.5 to 1 m zoomed in: roofs include the eaves, and tall things lean in aerial photos.

Archives near here

Tools > Archives near here (Explore) lists photographs, maps and plans, Public Works drawings (buildings, roads, bridges) and films in the Libraries Tasmania / Tasmanian Archives catalogue whose titles name a place near the pin (or the middle of the map). Nearby places with items show as brown dots and as buttons; pick one, filter by a word or year, and tap a title to open its catalogue page. Items are matched only by the place named in the title (a "?" marks a name that is also a common word or surname), so "near here" means "names this town or locality". Catalogue: data.gov.au (CC BY 4.0), open-access items, as of 2021. Copies of archive items are for private research; other uses need permission from Libraries Tasmania.

Share and print

  • The web address always holds the current view, basemap, layers and pin, so any link you copy or share opens the same map for someone else.
  • Print prints the current view (or saves it as PDF from the print dialog) with the pin panel, a header with basemap, date and extent, and the grid if it is on. It is a picture, not a georeferenced PDF.

Install on your phone

  • iPhone / iPad (Safari): Share, then Add to Home Screen.
  • Android (Chrome): the three-dot menu, then Add to Home screen or Install app.

It then opens full screen from its own icon, and works offline for saved areas.

Notes and accuracy

  • Coordinates are GDA2020, which matches GPS to well within a metre. MGA94 is converted with the standard 7-parameter transformation (about 0.15 m).
  • Coordinates: GDA2020 / GDA94 (in Tools) sets the datum for grid references, the MGA rows, the grid overlay, snapping, and how unlabelled eastings and northings are read in Go to and imports. GDA2020 is the default and matches phone GPS; switch to GDA94 to work with older maps and records. The two differ by about 1.5-2 m in Tasmania - no difference to a 6- or 8-figure grid reference, but it matters at 1 m. Map images (basemaps, aerial photos) are pictures and are not moved by the switch. The choice is kept on this device and printed in the header of a printed map.
  • Parcel boundaries are indicative only, not survey boundaries.
  • Data can be incomplete (for example gates and water assets on private land) - it is only as good as the public datasets behind it.
